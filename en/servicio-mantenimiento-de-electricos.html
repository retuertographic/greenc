    EV maintenance

    An EV skips many jobs a combustion car needs, but brakes, tyres, cooling, filters and electronics still need regular attention.

    Why service an EV

    Brakes that barely work

    Regenerative braking means the discs are used less, which makes them prone to rust and sticking if nobody checks them.

    Weight and tyres

    EVs are usually heavier and deliver torque instantly. Tyres and suspension feel it.

    Warranty and value

    Following the manufacturer's schedule helps keep the warranty and shows the car has been well looked after if you sell it.

    What's included

    We tailor the service to your model's maintenance schedule and to the mileage you do.

    Brakes

    Condition of discs and pads, calliper operation and a check of the brake fluid.

    Tyres, suspension and steering

    Wear, pressures, play and alignment, with extra attention to the car's weight.

    Cooling

    Level and condition of the cooling circuit, which on many models also controls battery temperature.

    Cabin filter and climate

    The filter that cleans the air you breathe and the operation of the air con or heat pump.

    Diagnostics and warnings

    Reading system warnings and checking the 12-volt battery.

    General safety

    Lights, wipers, fluid levels and the items checked at the ITV (MOT).

    Each manufacturer sets its own intervals and tasks. We'll tell you what's due once we've seen the car and its service book; prices are confirmed in the estimate.

    What changes compared with a combustion car

    An electric car has no engine oil change, spark plugs, timing belt or exhaust. That cuts maintenance, but doesn't remove it. Some parts keep working as before and others work differently.

    Brakes are the clearest example. Thanks to regenerative braking, the electric motor slows the car while recovering energy, and the discs are barely used. That sounds like a plus, and it is, but in salty air like the south of Tenerife's, discs that hardly work tend to rust and callipers can stick. Checking and cleaning them in time avoids surprises.

    What we check at each visit

    • Brakes: discs, pads, callipers and brake fluid, which absorbs moisture over time even if you brake little.
    • Tyres: wear, pressures and rotation where appropriate.
    • Suspension and steering: play and condition of shock absorbers and bushes.
    • Cooling circuit and coolant, as the manufacturer specifies.
    • Cabin filter and climate control operation.
    • 12-volt battery and a read-out of system warnings.

    Maintenance and warranty

    Just as with a combustion car, European rules allow you to have your car serviced at an independent workshop without losing the manufacturer's warranty, as long as its specifications are followed and suitable parts are used. We record the work done so your service history stays up to date.

    Tips for everyday driving on the island

    If you park in the sun every day, try to charge in the shade when you can, and avoid leaving the car for long periods at full or nearly empty charge if you won't be using it. Simple habits like these help look after the high-voltage battery. If your car sits unused for spells, ask us how best to leave it.

    Another thing we often look at is the charging cable you keep in the boot. It gets stepped on, bent and left in the sun, and a damaged or dirty connector can cause charging problems. If charging keeps stopping or the connector gets hotter than usual, mention it at the service. Sometimes the fault is in the charging point rather than the car.

    If you notice a fault, see electric vehicle repair. To arrange a service, book an appointment or ask for an estimate.

    Frequently asked questions

    How often should an EV be serviced?

    It depends on the manufacturer and model. The service book tells you; we check it with you and plan the service accordingly.

    Do I lose the warranty if I don't use the dealer?

    No, as long as the manufacturer's maintenance schedule is followed and suitable parts are used. That's what the European vehicle distribution rules provide.

    If I hardly use the brakes, why check them?

    Precisely because discs that work little rust and callipers can stick, especially near the sea. Brake fluid also ages over time.

    Do you also check the high-voltage battery?

    We read the information the car's systems provide using our diagnostic equipment, as far as each model allows, and talk you through what we see.

    Common misconceptions

    What people often believe, and what actually happens

    Six ideas we often hear at the garage that are worth clearing up.

    “If I don't go to the main dealer, I lose my warranty.”

    European rules let you service your car at an independent garage without losing the manufacturer's warranty, as long as its specifications are followed and suitable parts are used.

    “My insurer decides where my car is repaired.”

    As a rule you can choose the garage. Some policies offer perks at their partner garages: we help you read yours before you decide.

    “A small dent can wait.”

    A scrape that lifts the paint leaves bare metal exposed. With the sun and salty air in the south of the island, corrosion sets in sooner than you think.

    “Eco-friendly paint is lower quality.”

    A paint with fewer solvents can give an equally durable finish. What makes the difference is the preparation and the application.

    “An electric car doesn't need a garage.”

    It has fewer wearing parts, but brakes, tyres, suspension, air conditioning and battery cooling still need checking.

    “You only need to check the car before the MOT.”

    The ITV checks minimum standards at a given moment. Regular maintenance is what prevents costly breakdowns and nasty surprises on the road.

    Motoring glossary

    The terms you'll find in a garage estimate, an invoice or an insurance claim, explained in plain language.

    1ABCDEFHILMOPRSTVW

    1

    12-volt battery

    The battery that starts the engine and powers the car's electronics. Even electric cars have one. Heat shortens its lifespan.

    A

    ABS

    Anti-lock braking system. It stops the wheels locking under hard braking so you can still steer. If its warning light comes on, have it checked as soon as possible.

    Accident report form

    The Spanish 'Declaración Amistosa de Accidente': a form signed by the drivers involved describing how the accident happened, to help the insurers handle the claim.

    ADAS

    Advanced driver assistance systems: emergency braking, lane departure warning, adaptive cruise control and so on. They rely on cameras, radars and sensors.

    ADAS calibration

    Re-aligning the cameras and radars of driver assistance systems after replacing a windscreen or bumper, or after a knock, so they 'see' correctly again.

    Air conditioning

    The system that cools and dries the cabin air using a refrigerant gas and a compressor. It needs periodic checks for gas level, leaks and the cabin filter.

    Air filter

    The filter that cleans the air going into the engine. In dusty areas or during calima it clogs sooner; when blocked, the engine performs worse and uses more fuel.

    Airbag

    A bag that inflates in milliseconds during a crash to cushion the occupants. Once it has deployed, it must be replaced together with its related components.

    Antifreeze / coolant

    The fluid in the engine cooling system. It prevents overheating, freezing and internal corrosion. Its level should be checked and it should be replaced as the manufacturer specifies.

    Auxiliary belt

    The belt that drives the alternator, power steering pump or air conditioning compressor. If it snaps, the battery may stop charging or you may lose assistance.

    B

    Ball joint

    A joint in the steering and suspension that lets the wheel turn and move. If it has play, you will hear knocks and the steering will feel vaguer.

    Body filler

    A paste used in bodywork to fill small imperfections before painting. It is sanded to leave a perfectly smooth surface.

    Bodywork

    The car's outer shell: doors, wings, bonnet, roof, tailgate and bumpers. It protects the occupants and gives the vehicle its shape.

    Brake disc

    The metal disc that turns with the wheel and that the brake pads clamp to slow the car. It wears with use and has a marked minimum thickness.

    Brake fluid

    The fluid that transmits pedal pressure to the brakes. It absorbs moisture over time and loses effectiveness, so it is replaced periodically.

    Brake pads

    Parts with friction material that clamp the disc to slow the car. They wear with use; if they squeal or the warning light comes on, have them checked.

    Bushing (silentblock)

    A rubber bush that joins suspension or engine parts and absorbs vibration. Over time and with heat it cracks and noises appear.

    C

    Cabin filter

    The filter that cleans the air entering the cabin through the ventilation. It traps dust and pollen and helps prevent bad smells.

    Catalytic converter

    An exhaust component that converts part of the harmful gases into less harmful ones. It is key to passing the ITV (MOT).

    Charging connector

    The socket on an electric or plug-in hybrid car where the charging cable connects. In Europe the most common are Type 2 and CCS for rapid charging.

    Chassis

    The structure that supports the vehicle. In most cars it is built into the body (monocoque). If it is distorted in a crash, it has to be repaired on a chassis jig.

    Chassis jig

    Workshop equipment that holds the car and lets technicians measure and straighten the chassis or body structure after a heavy impact, back to the manufacturer's dimensions.

    Claim event

    Any insured event that causes damage: an accident, a knock while parked, broken glass, theft and so on.

    Clear coat

    The transparent layer applied over the colour coat. It adds gloss and protects the paint from sun, rain and light scratches.

    Clutch

    The mechanism that connects and disconnects the engine from the gearbox so you can pull away and change gear. It wears faster in stop-start traffic and on hills.

    Colour matching

    The technique of reproducing your car's exact colour, taking into account the factory code and how the paint has aged, so the repair blends in.

    Complaints form

    The official form every workshop must make available so customers can file a complaint with the consumer authorities.

    Comprehensive insurance

    Insurance that, as well as third-party damage, covers your own car even if you are at fault, subject to the agreed terms and excess.

    Corrosion

    Deterioration of metal through rust. Sea salt and coastal humidity speed it up, especially on the underbody, wheel arches and areas with damaged paint.

    Courtesy car

    A car lent to you while yours is in the workshop. Availability depends on the case and, for insurance claims, on your policy.

    D

    Diesel particulate filter (DPF)

    A filter in diesel exhausts that traps soot. It cleans itself (regenerates) on longer journeys; lots of short trips can clog it.

    Dual-mass flywheel

    A two-piece flywheel linked by springs that absorbs vibration, common in diesels. When worn, you get knocking and vibration when starting or stopping.

    E

    EGR valve

    A valve that sends part of the exhaust gases back into the intake to reduce emissions. Over time it builds up carbon and can cause faults.

    Electric vehicle

    A car driven solely by electric motors powered by a battery you charge from a socket. It has fewer wearing parts than a combustion car.

    Engine control unit (ECU)

    The car's computer that manages the engine and other systems. It stores fault codes that the workshop reads with diagnostic equipment.

    Equivalent-quality part

    A part from another manufacturer that meets the same quality and specifications as the original. It is often a more affordable option.

    Excess

    The part of a claim's cost that you pay under your policy. The insurer covers the rest up to the agreed limits.

    F

    Free choice of repairer

    Your right to have your car repaired at the workshop of your choice, even when the damage is covered by insurance. It is worth checking the specific terms of your policy.

    H

    High-voltage battery

    The main battery in an electric or hybrid car, which drives the electric motor. It runs at hundreds of volts, so only trained staff with the right equipment should work on it.

    Hybrid

    A car that combines a combustion engine with one or more electric motors. The battery recharges as you drive, with no need to plug in.

    I

    Injector

    The part that sprays fuel into the engine in the right amount at the right moment. A dirty or faulty injector causes hesitation, smoke or higher consumption.

    ITV (MOT)

    Spain's official vehicle inspection (similar to the UK MOT). It periodically checks that the car is safe and within emission limits.

    L

    Lambda sensor

    An exhaust sensor that measures oxygen in the gases so the ECU can adjust the mixture. If it fails, consumption and emissions rise.

    Loss adjuster

    A professional who assesses a vehicle's damage and repair cost, usually on behalf of the insurer. You can also hire an independent adjuster.

    Loss assessment

    A loss adjuster's assessment of a vehicle's damage after a claim event: what is repaired or replaced, what it costs and whether repair makes sense.

    M

    Metallic paint

    Paint containing tiny metallic particles that reflect light and give a distinctive sparkle. It is trickier to match in a partial repair.

    O

    OBD diagnostics

    Reading information from the ECU through the car's OBD port. It shows fault codes and live data to help pinpoint the problem.

    Oil filter

    The filter that traps dirt and metal particles in the engine oil. It is replaced along with the oil.

    Original part

    A part made by or for the car manufacturer, identical to the one fitted at the factory.

    P

    Paint booth

    An enclosed booth with filtered air and temperature control where the car is painted and cured free of dust, for a clean finish.

    Paintless dent repair

    A technique that pushes the panel back into shape from behind, with no filler or paint. It suits small dents where the paint is undamaged.

    Pearlescent paint

    Paint with pigments that shift tone depending on the angle of the light. It is usually applied in several layers and needs great precision to match.

    Plug-in hybrid (PHEV)

    A hybrid with a larger battery that you charge from a socket. It can cover a certain distance on electric power alone.

    Polishing

    A treatment that removes a very thin layer of clear coat to get rid of light scratches, marks and dullness, bringing the shine back.

    Primer

    A preparation coat applied before the colour. It helps the paint adhere and protects the metal against corrosion.

    R

    Regenerative braking

    A feature of electric and hybrid cars that uses braking to recharge the battery. As well as saving energy, it reduces wear on pads and discs.

    Repair estimate

    A document setting out the work to be done and its cost before starting. You are entitled to ask for it in writing and to accept it or not.

    Repair order

    The document authorising the workshop to carry out specific work. It records what was agreed with you and serves as a reference if any question arises.

    Repair warranty

    Cover for work done by the workshop. In Spain the regulations set a general minimum of 3 months or 2,000 km for repairs; some jobs may carry a longer warranty.

    S

    Shock absorber

    A suspension component that damps the spring's bouncing and keeps the tyre on the road. Worn shock absorbers lengthen braking distances and reduce stability.

    Spark plug

    The part that creates the spark to ignite the air-fuel mixture in petrol engines. When worn, the engine loses power and uses more fuel.

    Suspension

    The springs, shock absorbers, arms and joints that connect the wheels to the body. It provides comfort, grip and stability.

    T

    Third-party insurance

    Insurance that covers, at minimum, damage you cause to other people and vehicles. Damage to your own car is normally not covered if you are at fault.

    Timing belt

    The belt that keeps the crankshaft and camshafts in time. It is replaced at the mileage or age the manufacturer sets: if it breaks, engine damage can be severe.

    Total loss

    When repairing the car would cost more than it is worth under the policy or in the loss adjuster's view. The insurer pays out instead of repairing.

    Turbocharger

    A compressor driven by exhaust gases that forces more air into the engine for extra power. It appreciates good oil and not switching off straight after hard driving.

    Tyre

    The only part of the car that touches the road. Keep an eye on pressure, tread depth (legal minimum 1.6 mm) and age, as sun and heat make tyres deteriorate.

    V

    Vehicle deposit receipt

    The document the workshop gives you when you leave the car, with its details and condition. It is your proof for collecting it.

    Volatile organic compounds (VOC)

    Substances that evaporate from solvents and paints and pollute the air. Low-VOC paints, such as water-based ones, cut these emissions.

    W

    Warning light

    A dashboard light warning of a problem. If it is red, stop as soon as it is safe; if it is amber, get it checked soon.

    Water-based paint

    Paint that uses water rather than chemical solvents as its main carrier. It releases far fewer volatile organic compounds and is kinder to the environment.

    Wheel alignment

    Adjusting the wheel angles so they run straight and parallel. Poor alignment wears tyres unevenly and makes the car pull to one side.

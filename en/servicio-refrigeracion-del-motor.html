    Engine cooling

    If the temperature gauge climbs higher than usual, you're losing coolant or the fan never stops, get the cooling system checked before the engine suffers.

    Why look after the cooling system

    Heat and air conditioning

    High temperatures, motorway queues and the air con on full push the system to its limits.

    Climbing Teide

    A long, slow climb with a loaded car is where cooling faults appear that go unnoticed on the flat.

    Overheating is costly

    Driving with an overheated engine can damage the head gasket or cylinder head. Stopping in time makes all the difference.

    What's included

    We check the whole circuit, not just the level in the reservoir.

    Coolant

    Condition and concentration of the fluid. Changed following the manufacturer's schedule with the type your engine requires.

    Pressure test

    We pressurise the circuit to find leaks in hoses, radiator, pump or cap.

    Thermostat and sensors

    Checking the thermostat opens on time and the temperature sensor reads correctly.

    Fans

    Operation of the cooling fan, its speeds, relays and fuses.

    Water pump and radiator

    Inspection of the water pump, radiator and heater matrix.

    Electronic diagnostics

    Live temperature readings and stored fault codes from the ECU.

    If there are signs of head gasket damage, we explain the tests we've carried out and give you a separate estimate, as it's a much bigger job.

    The system that keeps the engine at the right temperature

    An engine needs to run at a specific temperature: neither too cold nor too hot. Coolant flows through the block, picks up heat and releases it in the radiator. The thermostat regulates the flow, the water pump moves it and the fan helps when the car is moving slowly or stationary. If just one of them fails, the temperature can shoot up.

    Heat, traffic and the climb up Teide

    Several factors come together in the south of Tenerife: plenty of heat for much of the year, queues on the TF-1 and the air conditioning running, which also adds heat in front of the radiator. Then there's the climb up to Teide: many kilometres uphill, often behind other cars at low speed. That's exactly when a partly blocked radiator, a sluggish thermostat or a fan that doesn't kick in gets found out.

    • Before setting off, check the expansion tank level with the engine cold.
    • Keep an eye on the temperature gauge on the way up.
    • If it starts rising, switch off the air con and turn the heater to maximum: it helps draw heat away from the engine.
    • If it reaches the red or the warning light comes on, stop somewhere safe and switch off.
    • Never open the reservoir cap with a hot engine: the coolant is under pressure.

    How we check it

    We start with electronic diagnostics to see real temperatures and any stored faults. Then we pressurise the circuit to find leaks, test the thermostat and fans and inspect the radiator, whose fins often suffer corrosion from the salty air on the coast. We also look at the fluid itself: over time it loses its protection against internal corrosion, which is why the manufacturer specifies when to change it.

    On many engines the water pump is driven by the timing belt. If that's the case with yours and the timing belt is due soon, it makes sense to do both together.

    Once we've diagnosed the problem, you get a written estimate. If you've broken down after overheating, call your roadside assistance (see our assistance numbers) and have the car brought to the workshop. If you've only noticed symptoms, book an appointment and we'll check it before your next trip up the mountain.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I top up with tap water?

    In an emergency, to get to the workshop, yes. But water alone doesn't protect against corrosion or overheating. The correct coolant must be restored afterwards.

    I'm losing coolant but can't see any puddles. Where is it going?

    It may be evaporating from a small leak that dries in the heat, or getting into the combustion chamber. A pressure test and diagnostics will tell us.

    How often should coolant be changed?

    According to the manufacturer's schedule, which depends on the type of fluid. We'll check it at your service.

    White smoke and a sweet smell from the exhaust. Is that serious?

    It may mean coolant is getting into the engine. It's best not to keep using the car and to have it checked as soon as possible.

    The fan keeps running after I switch off. Is that normal?

    On many models, yes, for a few minutes after hard use. If it never stops or doesn't come on when it should, we'll check it.

    Common misconceptions

    What people often believe, and what actually happens

    Six ideas we often hear at the garage that are worth clearing up.

    “If I don't go to the main dealer, I lose my warranty.”

    European rules let you service your car at an independent garage without losing the manufacturer's warranty, as long as its specifications are followed and suitable parts are used.

    “My insurer decides where my car is repaired.”

    As a rule you can choose the garage. Some policies offer perks at their partner garages: we help you read yours before you decide.

    “A small dent can wait.”

    A scrape that lifts the paint leaves bare metal exposed. With the sun and salty air in the south of the island, corrosion sets in sooner than you think.

    “Eco-friendly paint is lower quality.”

    A paint with fewer solvents can give an equally durable finish. What makes the difference is the preparation and the application.

    “An electric car doesn't need a garage.”

    It has fewer wearing parts, but brakes, tyres, suspension, air conditioning and battery cooling still need checking.

    “You only need to check the car before the MOT.”

    The ITV checks minimum standards at a given moment. Regular maintenance is what prevents costly breakdowns and nasty surprises on the road.

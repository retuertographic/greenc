    Vehicle loss assessment, explained

    After reporting a claim, the next step is usually the loss adjuster's visit. Understanding how it works helps you know what to expect and protect your interests.

    What a loss assessment is

    A loss assessment is the technical valuation of the damage to your vehicle. It is carried out by a loss adjuster, usually appointed by the insurer, who decides which parts are repaired or replaced, how many labour hours are needed and what the repair will cost in total.

    Based on that assessment, the insurer authorises the repair or, if the cost is very high compared with the car's value, may propose a total loss.

    How it works

    1. You report the claim and choose a workshop.
    2. The insurer appoints an adjuster and the workshop arranges the visit.
    3. The adjuster inspects the car at the workshop, takes photos and reviews the visible damage.
    4. If hidden damage appears once parts are removed, the workshop reports it and the assessment is extended.
    5. The insurer authorises the repair according to the adjuster's report.

    Sometimes the assessment is done from photos or video sent by the workshop. Timing depends on the insurer's and adjuster's workload, so it is not wise to promise fixed dates; what does help is having the car available and your paperwork in order.

    What the adjuster checks

    • Bodywork damage: panels, bumpers, glass and lights.
    • Any structural distortion that needs a chassis jig.
    • Affected mechanical parts: suspension, steering, radiator.
    • Safety and driver assistance systems that need checking or calibrating.
    • Whether the damage matches the reported incident.

    If you disagree

    If you think the assessment falls short or misses some damage, talk to the workshop first: often extra photos or documents are enough for the adjuster to review the report. If you still disagree, Spain's Insurance Contract Act allows you to appoint your own adjuster. Bear in mind that you may have to pay their fees, depending on the case.

    How to prepare

    • Have your claim reference and policy details to hand.
    • Keep the photos you took at the time of the incident and the accident report form.
    • Note any symptoms you noticed after the impact: noises, vibrations, warning lights.
    • Tell the workshop about any previous damage the car already had, so it is not mixed up with the new damage.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need to be there when the adjuster comes?

    No. If the car is at our workshop, we arrange the visit and let you know the outcome.

    Can the repair start before the assessment?

    Usually not, because the insurer needs to see the damage before authorising the work. If something urgent is needed to move the car safely, we check with the insurer.

    What if more damage appears when parts are removed?

    That is common. We document it with photos and ask for the assessment to be extended before carrying on.

    Does the adjuster work for me or for the insurer?

    An adjuster appointed by the insurer works on its behalf, although they must apply technical judgement. If you are not satisfied, you can hire an independent adjuster.

    Can I use my car while I wait for the adjuster?

    If the damage is minor and the car is safe, sometimes yes. But if lights are broken, you hear noises or a warning light is on, it is better not to drive and to leave it at the workshop until the visit.

    Auto electrics

    An electrical fault can be as simple as a fuse or as hidden as a chafed wire behind a trim panel. We track it down methodically and explain what we find.

    Why not ignore an electrical fault

    Safety and legal

    Lights, indicators, wipers and horn are not extras: they are compulsory and checked at the ITV (MOT).

    Drains that flatten the battery

    A module that never goes to sleep or a wire shorting to earth can leave you unable to start after a few days parked.

    Avoid bigger damage

    A short circuit or an overheating connection can affect other components. Better to check it early.

    What's included

    We go through the electrical system in an orderly way, from the simplest to the most hidden.

    Lighting and signalling

    Headlights, rear lights, indicators, brake and number plate lights, with their connections and bulb holders.

    Comfort and access

    Electric windows, central locking, electric mirrors, switches and controls.

    Parasitic drain

    Wiring and connectors

    Charging and starting

    We check the alternator, starter motor and main connections.

    If the fault is logged in a control unit, we use that code as our starting point.

    Intermittent electrical faults can take time to find. Before going beyond the initial check, we let you know and agree it with you.

    The most common electrical faults

    Most electrical jobs we see follow a pattern: a light that won't come on, a window that stops halfway, central locking that fails on one door, or a 12-volt battery that is flat in the morning for no obvious reason. Behind it there may be something as simple as a bulb or a fuse, or something more hidden like a broken wire in a door hinge.

    In the south of Tenerife there is an extra factor: the sea air. Salt encourages [[corrosion]] in connectors and earth points, especially on cars parked outside near the coast. A corroded earth can cause odd faults in several systems at once.

    How we track the fault down

    We start with the basics: fuses, relays, battery voltage and earths. If the affected circuit is managed by a control unit, we read its codes with our diagnostic equipment. Then we follow the circuit with a multimeter until we find the exact point of failure. That way we avoid replacing a window motor when the real problem is a connector.

    • Check of fuses, relays and main earth points.
    • Measurement of current draw with the car switched off.
    • Inspection of wiring looms where they flex: doors, tailgate, under seats.
    • Cleaning or replacement of corroded connectors.
    • Repair of badly wired accessories.

    Accessories and previous installations

    Alarms, cameras, tow bars or sound systems fitted carelessly are a frequent source of trouble: joins wrapped in tape, wires without a fuse or power sockets that stay live all the time. If your car has any of these add-ons and you notice faults, tell us; it really helps to focus the search.

    A final note on the ITV (MOT): the inspection checks that all compulsory lights, the horn, the wipers and the dashboard warning lights work. A blown rear bulb or an indicator flashing too fast can count as a defect. These are simple repairs worth sorting out before the test, and we take the chance to check that the connections are in good condition so they don't fail again a few days later.

    If the problem is that the car won't start, see our batteries and starting page too. For anything else, get in touch or book an appointment.

    Frequently asked questions

    My battery goes flat if the car sits for a few days. Is it the battery?

    It could be the battery, but it could also be a parasitic drain. We measure both before recommending a replacement.

    Do you fix accessory wiring done by another garage?

    Yes. We check the installation and, if there are unsafe joins or connections, we suggest redoing it properly.

    A bulb has blown. Do I need an appointment?

    For something simple, call or WhatsApp us and we'll tell you when to drop by. If the headlight has to come out, we'll arrange an appointment.

    Can damp or salty air cause electrical faults?

    Yes. Corrosion on connectors and earths is a common cause of intermittent faults, especially on cars kept near the sea.

    Common misconceptions

    What people often believe, and what actually happens

    Six ideas we often hear at the garage that are worth clearing up.

    “If I don't go to the main dealer, I lose my warranty.”

    European rules let you service your car at an independent garage without losing the manufacturer's warranty, as long as its specifications are followed and suitable parts are used.

    “My insurer decides where my car is repaired.”

    As a rule you can choose the garage. Some policies offer perks at their partner garages: we help you read yours before you decide.

    “A small dent can wait.”

    A scrape that lifts the paint leaves bare metal exposed. With the sun and salty air in the south of the island, corrosion sets in sooner than you think.

    “Eco-friendly paint is lower quality.”

    A paint with fewer solvents can give an equally durable finish. What makes the difference is the preparation and the application.

    “An electric car doesn't need a garage.”

    It has fewer wearing parts, but brakes, tyres, suspension, air conditioning and battery cooling still need checking.

    “You only need to check the car before the MOT.”

    The ITV checks minimum standards at a given moment. Regular maintenance is what prevents costly breakdowns and nasty surprises on the road.
